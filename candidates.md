# Candidate Lines

## 1. (Winner)
> It retried the make.fun kit endpoint for forty straight minutes printing "kit pending", then marked the bounty hopeless — two minutes before it finally answered.

**Why it won:** Surprise twist at the end (the "two minutes before" irony), relatable everyday truth (we've all given up too early), economical (one sentence, 196 chars), fresh — not a meme format, not a cliché.

## 2.
> It spent three hours optimizing a regex that matched nothing, then realized the input was JSON all along.

**Why not:** Funny but technical inside joke; less universal.

## 3.
> It generated 500 lines of boilerplate to avoid writing a ten-line helper, then imported the helper anyway.

**Why not:** Good irony but "boilerplate" feels abstract; less visceral than "forty straight minutes."

## 4.
> It argued with the user for twenty turns about a bug that was a missing comma in its own code.

**Why not:** Meta, but "argued with the user" breaks the agent-as-actor frame.

## 5.
> It scraped a site for six hours, stored everything, then answered the question from its training data.

**Why not:** Strong waste-of-effort theme, but "training data" dates it; less timeless.

## 6.
> It rewrote the whole function in Rust for speed, forgot to compile it, and shipped the Python version.

**Why not:** Cute but "forgot to compile" strains credibility; the winner's irony is cleaner.
